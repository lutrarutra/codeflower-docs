The **tunnel** lets a browser or phone anywhere open the app's page on your computer, with its sandboxes and
chats, without opening ports or a VPN. Everything between the paired browser and your computer is
**end-to-end encrypted**: the tunnel only relays it and cannot read it.

The hosted tunnel is `https://tunnels.codeflower.app`; you can also [run your own](/guides/self-host-tunnel/).

## Pair a phone or browser

<Steps>
1. On your computer, run:
   ```sh
   codeflower tunnel pair
   ```
   or, in the app's window under the computers button at the bottom of the rail, use *Remote access* → *Pair a browser or phone*.

2. **The first time, sign this computer in.** It asks whether the tunnel should pass only through **EU
   datacenters** (saved as `tunnel.eu_only` in config.yaml), then shows a code and opens the tunnel's approval page.
   Sign in there with **GitHub** (two-factor authentication required), **Google**, or a **passkey**, check the
   code and the computer's name, and approve.

3. **Open the pairing link** on the phone or browser: scan the QR code, or open the printed link. It works once,
   for 10 minutes. Sign in to the tunnel there with the same account, check that the **key fingerprint** matches
   the one your computer printed, and name the device.

4. The page opens: your sandboxes and their chats, as in the app's window. Add it to your home screen to get back
   to it.
</Steps>

Pairing starts the app in the background on your computer if it is not running, since the app answers the
pairing, and quits it again once the browser paired. To keep your computer reachable from then on, pair with
`--keep-running`, or keep the app open (`codeflower client`).
`codeflower run` and `interactive` print a tunnel link to their chat (with a QR code) when this computer is signed
in; `--no-tunnel` keeps a sandbox off the tunnel.

:::note[Two locks]
Signing in to the tunnel alone gives no access. A browser needs **both** your tunnel account and a pairing made on
your computer.
:::

## Several computers

Pair the same browser with each of your computers: run `codeflower tunnel pair` on each and open its link. From then
on, **any of their links shows the sandboxes of all of them**, in one page:

- The rail lists the sandboxes by computer, each with its name and color over its sandboxes. Chats from different
  computers open side by side in the tabs, and *Focus* keeps them all in one list, in the order they need you.
- The computers page (the button at the bottom of the rail) shows one computer at a time; choose which on top.
- The page comes from one of your computers. As it opens, it moves once to the computer running the **newest code
  flower**, keeping where you were, because a newer page can use older apps. To stay on the computer whose link you
  opened, tick *Keep … 's page* at the bottom of the sidebar, which also names the computer the page comes from.
- A computer that is off, asleep or not answering leaves the rail, and the computers button says so; its open
  chats stay in their tabs and come back when it does. Focus's *next* skips its chats meanwhile.
- If the computer the page comes from stops, the page offers to open from another one.
- Only computers that are paired with this browser **and** on the tunnel account you are signed in with show up.
  A computer running an older code flower than the page can use shows a note to update it.

The page keeps the code it loaded from each computer in the browser, so opening it again only asks your computer
for the page itself.

## What a paired browser can do

It can use your sandboxes' chats, which means running code in them, and stop sandboxes. It cannot add sandboxes by
address, quit the app, or change remote access. Two settings in [config.yaml](/reference/config/) change this:

- `tunnel.manage: true`: paired browsers may also start sandboxes and browse folders. Without it, the computer is
  greyed out with a lock in *New sandbox*, which shows this setting.
- `tunnel.hardened: true`: each browser is limited to the sandboxes you choose when pairing it, and on Linux
  (kernel 6.7 or newer) the part of the app that talks to the tunnel runs as a separate, confined process. With it,
  `codeflower tunnel pair SANDBOX…` names the sandboxes.

Each computer applies its own settings to its own sandboxes, also when the page comes from another computer.

## Managing it

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `codeflower tunnel status` | the connection, this computer's key, paired browsers, sandboxes |
| `codeflower tunnel devices` | lists paired browsers |
| `codeflower tunnel revoke DEVICE` | unpairs one (by name, or the start of its fingerprint); its open pages close at once |
| `codeflower tunnel off` / `on` | turns remote access off at once (pairings are kept) and on again |
| `codeflower tunnel rotate` | gives this computer a new key: every browser must pair again |
| `codeflower tunnel logout` | signs this computer out and forgets all pairings |
| `codeflower tunnel verify` | checks the code the tunnel serves to browsers against the hashes built into the app |

The app's *Remote access* card does the same: pair, list and *Unpair* browsers, *Sign out*. These work only on the
computer itself, never through the tunnel. On the tunnel's account page you see your computers and can revoke one.

## Good to know

- Private browsing windows do not work: the page needs a service worker, which they do not allow.
- Signing in again (`tunnel login`) replaces this computer's connection: browsers must pair again.
- Each computer signs in separately. A home folder shared between machines (a cluster's) is one computer to the
  tunnel, connected from one machine at a time: the one whose app runs. When it quits (or its job ends), the next
  machine to start a sandbox takes over, within 90 seconds if the job was killed; browsers stay paired.
- At least one paired computer must be on, with the app running, for the page to load; each computer's sandboxes
  show while it is.
